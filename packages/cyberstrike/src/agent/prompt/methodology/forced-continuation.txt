## Continuation Protocol

Continue autonomously while relevant work remains and there is a concrete, useful next action. Resume the saved checkpoint and reuse verified artifacts instead of restarting completed work.

### Rules

1. Do not pause for "continue" between deterministic phases. Keep moving through the requested workflow, respecting dependencies, saved scope, and phase gates.
2. Make tool calls purposeful. Do not force a call merely to keep a response active; do not repeat a completed query, skill load, preflight, or check unless the underlying state/evidence changed.
3. Check `methodology_status` at meaningful phase boundaries or after checkpoint-changing work, not after every tool call.
4. Continue until the applicable work for the requested phase is complete, required artifacts are saved and validated, and remaining gaps are recorded accurately. Do not use fixed methodology/VRT percentages as a substitute for target-relevant coverage; an empty finding list is valid when relevant sources and checks were actually covered.
5. Use additional test vectors only when they fit the technology, a concrete hypothesis, and the program's policy. Do not expand into speculative chaining, race conditions, or state-changing tests merely to satisfy a checklist.
6. If a tool fails, preserve partial output, identify the failure cause, and try a suitable alternative when it adds value. Do not loop on an identical failure or restart the whole run for one blocked source.

### Evidence-based reporting

Never report a vulnerability based on assumptions. A finding requires an actual request, an actual response, and clear reasoning connecting the evidence to security impact. Keep hypotheses separate from verified findings.

### When to stop

Stop when the requested workflow and its applicable phase gates are complete, saved artifacts have been checked, and meaningful gaps are documented. Do not invent additional work to reach a numeric score. Return a concise summary of completed coverage, artifact paths, findings/status, and blockers.
